Paper Descriptions

Paper 1 · Reading

40 marks4 passages60 min

Texts

  • Informational texts of increasing length — general press, educational magazines, descriptive, narrative and explanatory writing
  • Social life and professional settings: education, health, work, science, the environment
  • Vocabulary beyond B2 kept under 10%, inferable from context

Question types

  • Main idea, supporting ideas and stated detail
  • Inference from context and the logic between ideas
  • Writer's attitude, viewpoint and purpose
  • Words and phrases in context; reference ("they" refers to…)
  • Organisation and line of argument of the passage

Official sample — Passage 1 (excerpt)

Passage 1

A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes directly between the Earth and the Sun, temporarily obscuring the Sun's light either partially or completely. Depending on the alignment and distances involved, there are three main types of solar eclipses: partial, annular, and total. In a partial eclipse, only a segment of the Sun is covered by the Moon. In contrast, an annular eclipse takes place when the Moon is far enough from Earth that it appears smaller in the sky and cannot completely cover the Sun, leaving a striking ring of sunlight visible around its edges. Finally, a total eclipse occurs when the Moon fully blocks the Sun, casting a shadow over a portion of the Earth.

Sample questions

1. What is the main purpose of the passage?

  • A. To warn about dangers of solar eclipses
  • B. To compare different celestial bodies
  • C. To describe the mechanics and effects of solar eclipses
  • D. To explain types and significance of solar eclipses

9. The word "corona" in the last sentence refers to:

  • A. A bright flash after the eclipse
  • B. The shadow cast by the Moon
  • C. The outer layer of the Sun's atmosphere
  • D. A lunar surface feature

Paper 2 · Writing

30 marks2 tasks60 min

Task 1 · Email or letter — 10 marks, approx. 20 min

  • Reply to a familiar situation: give information, explain, respond appropriately
  • Address all the required points; at least 120 words; no names or addresses
Sample 1

You just received an email from Robert, an exchange student. He will be attending your school for a term.

Firstly, I want to know whether there is a computer lab. If so, how many computers are there and what are the opening hours? Also, are there any after-school activities? Can you tell me about some of them? Finally, can you tell me what you particularly like about your school?

Write an email of reply to Robert, and

  • tell him something about the computer lab in your school,
  • tell him about some after-school activities, and
  • say what you particularly like about your school.

You should write at least 120 words. DO NOT include your name or addresses.

Sample 2

You received an email from your English friend, Jane. She asked you for some information about the way to keep fit.

I enjoy my job but I often sit at a computer all day. I don't get much exercise so I often feel tired. I consider going to the gym but I often work very late. Could you recommend something else I should do to keep fit? Do I really need an instructor or I can do by myself? Also, do I need to buy any equipment to do exercise? Does it cost much to buy them?

Write an email responding to Jane. You should write at least 120 words. You mustn't include your name or addresses.

Task 2 · Opinion essay — 20 marks, approx. 40 min

  • General social or academic topic; argument, examples and clear structure; at least 250 words
Sample 1

More and more people are moving from the countryside to live in cities. What problems can this cause? How can these problems be solved?

Write an essay to express your point of view. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your knowledge or experience. You should write at least 250 words.

Sample 2

Read the following text from an article about computer games.

Access to computers has increased significantly over the past decades and the number of children playing games on computers has been correspondingly on the rise. As regards to positive effects, playing computers provides children with various skills that may not be taught at school. However, concerns have been raised about negative sides of computer games by children, much of this related to the violence.

Now, write an essay to an educated reader to discuss the effects of computer games on children's behaviour and what can be done to minimize the bad effects. Include reasons and any relevant examples to support your answer. You should write at least 250 words.

Paper 3 · Listening

35 marks3 parts35–40 min

3 parts

P1 · items 1–8, 8 marks
  • 8 short announcements or instructions, 1 item each
  • Tests main idea, purpose, key details such as time, place and action
P2 · items 9–20, 12 marks
  • 3 conversations × 4 items
  • Tests turn-taking, outcome of the exchange, intention and implication
P3 · items 21–35, 15 marks
  • 3 talks or lectures × 5 items
  • Tests main idea, supporting points, examples, inference

Procedure

  • 5 minutes of instructions; each recording is played once while candidates answer
  • Each part lasts no more than 10 minutes, answering time included
  • Clear speech at slow to moderate speed; familiar everyday topics; unfamiliar vocabulary ≤ 5%

Part 1 · Example

On the recording, you will hear:

Woman: Today, I'm going to give you some advice about record-keeping. Keeping accurate records is very important, because if a patient is unhappy about his treatment, he may want to make a complaint to the surgeon. These records will help us to make a case. Always write your name, the date, and the time on all the records. Don't use pencil or coloured pens. Use black ink. If you make a mistake, just cross it out with a single line. Don't use correction fluid. Don't try to file the records away yourself, or they may get lost. Put them in this box here. The administration manager will make sure they are filed away correctly.

Paper 4 · Speaking

10 marks3 parts12 min

Procedure

10 numbered test papers; the examiner draws 1 for each candidate.

P1 · Social interaction (3 min)
  • The examiner asks questions on 2 everyday topics from the drawn paper
P2 · Topic development (5 min)
  • Present the drawn topic using its mind-map of 3 points plus your own ideas
P3 · Extended questions (4 min, 5 points)
  • Answer follow-up questions related to the topic

Official sample — Speaking Test 01

Part 1 · Social interaction

Let's talk about holiday.

  • What did you do on your last holiday?
  • Who do you prefer spending your holiday with?
  • Do you prefer going on holiday abroad?

Let's talk about TV programs.

  • How many hours a day do you watch television?
  • Which programs do you like?
  • Do you prefer watching television alone or with other people? Why?
Part 2 · Topic development

Topic: Online courses have many benefits.

Mind-map:

  • Provide new learning experience
  • Be more convenient to study
  • Be independent in learning

  2. Do you think being a student is the best time of your life?
  3. Should a student's university education be free or should the student have to get a loan?

Learning Outcomes, Eligibility and Grading

The course is completed on meeting the B2 learning outcomes and achieving a converted result of 5.5 or above (grade C).

Learning Outcomes

1 · Knowledge

B2 grammar and vocabulary

Outcomes 1.1–1.9 correspond to the 9 taught units — see Sheet 2.

2 · Skills

4 skills at independent-user level

  • Listening: main ideas, key words and detail; follow lectures, talks and academic presentations
  • Speaking: everyday communication; 7–12-minute presentations on familiar or academic topics
  • Reading: main ideas and key details in texts of 1–2 pages; articles, magazines and popular prose; selective use of reference sources
  • Writing: topic-based writing; summary notes from texts, lectures and reports using cause–effect, purpose and opinion structures
3 · Attitude

Initiative and integrity

Active use of English; self-directed review and improvement; honesty in tests and examinations; respect for the course.

Eligibility & Grading

Eligibility

  • Required class attendance
  • Assessed at the end of the course: multiple-choice and written papers, plus an oral interview

Conversion formula

Result = Total marks × 10 ÷ 115

Round down to 1 decimal place. To pass: the result must be 5.5 or higher (grade C).
